Clause 4 · Irrevocability

The hard part, before the good part.

Most products in this category tell you what you gain. An irrevocable trust is defined by what you give up, so that is what we put first.

  • 01
    The money stops being yours

    The day the deed is signed, the corpus belongs to a separate legal entity. Not to you, and not to us.

  • 02
    You cannot undo it

    No cooling-off, no emergency clause, no exception for a change of heart. If you might need the money, choose Seed instead — that is a real answer.

  • 03
    The payout plan is frozen at signing

    You choose how your child receives it once, in advance. That choice outlives your opinion of it.

The plain answers

The questions parents actually ask.

What if I need the money?
Then a trust is not for you yet, and that is a real answer rather than a deflection. TFB Seed keeps everything flexible — stop it, pause it or withdraw whenever you want. Come back to the Deed when you are certain.
Why not just PPF or Sukanya?
Both are good, and you should probably have them. They are also still yours — you can close a PPF. A trust is a separate legal entity: the money stops being yours the day the deed is signed. That is the only meaningful difference, and it is the whole point.
If it's free to start, how do you make money?
Trail commission from the fund houses on Seed and Harvest, the one-time ₹20,000 fee on a Deed, and an ITR filing fee where a trust or an SWP makes annual filing necessary. We do not charge a management or advisory fee.
How much will it grow to?
We don't project returns, and we'd gently suggest being wary of anyone who does. What we can tell you exactly is what you have contributed, who contributed it, and how many years remain. That is what the year ledger shows.
What happens if Trust Fund Baby shuts down?
The trust is a legal entity independent of us, and the investments sit with the asset management companies and their registrars — not with us. If we disappeared tomorrow, the trust would still exist.
